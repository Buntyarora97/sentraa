What Exactly Is a Chashma Hatane Ka Operation?

The phrase "chashma hatane ka operation" is the common Hindi and Hinglish term used by thousands of patients across Mumbai when they refer to surgical procedures designed to permanently eliminate the need for spectacles. In clinical terminology, this is known as refractive surgery — a family of procedures that reshape the cornea or implant a corrective lens inside the eye so that light focuses sharply on the retina without the aid of external glasses or contact lenses.

For decades, the only way to correct refractive errors such as myopia (nearsightedness), hyperopia (farsightedness), and astigmatism was to wear prescription lenses. While glasses and contacts remain effective, they come with daily inconveniences — fogging in humid Mumbai weather, discomfort during sports and swimming, recurring costs of replacement, and the simple frustration of misplacing them. Refractive surgery addresses the root cause of blurred vision by permanently altering the optical properties of the eye itself.

Modern refractive surgery has evolved dramatically since the earliest radial keratotomy procedures of the 1970s. Today, surgeons use precision excimer and femtosecond lasers controlled by advanced computer mapping of each patient's cornea. The result is a level of accuracy measured in microns — far beyond what any manual technique could achieve. At Sentra Clinic, we offer the full spectrum of these technologies, and we select the one best suited to your specific eye anatomy, prescription, and lifestyle.

A comprehensive eye examination is the first step toward your specs-free life.

Types of Chashma Hatane Ka Operation Available at Sentra Clinic

There is no single "best" refractive surgery — there is only the best procedure for your particular eyes. Your corneal thickness, pupil size, tear film quality, prescription stability, and lifestyle all determine which technique will give you the safest and sharpest outcome. At Sentra Clinic Malad East, we offer four major categories of specs removal surgery, each explained below.

👁️

Blade-Free LASIK

The most widely performed refractive procedure worldwide. A femtosecond laser creates a thin corneal flap, an excimer laser reshapes the underlying cornea, and the flap is repositioned. Recovery is rapid — most patients see 6/6 or better within 24 hours. Ideal for myopia up to -8 dioptres and moderate astigmatism.

✨

Contoura Vision

Topography-guided LASIK that maps 22,000 unique points on your cornea, treating not just your prescription but the microscopic irregularities unique to your eye. Often delivers sharper vision than glasses ever did — many patients achieve better than 6/6. Particularly beneficial for patients with astigmatism or irregular corneas.

🌟

SMILE Surgery

Small Incision Lenticule Extraction is a flapless, minimally invasive procedure where a femtosecond laser creates a tiny lens-shaped disc (lenticule) inside the cornea, removed through a 2-4 mm incision. Less corneal nerve disruption means faster tear film recovery and greater structural integrity — ideal for active patients and contact lens wearers.

🔬

ICL Implantation

Implantable Collamer Lens surgery is a lens-based alternative for patients whose prescriptions are too high or whose corneas are too thin for laser procedures. A biocompatible lens is placed inside the eye behind the iris. Reversible, UV-protective, and capable of correcting up to -20 dioptres of myopia.

👵

Refractive Lens Exchange

For patients over 45 who are developing presbyopia (reading glasses dependence), the eye's natural lens is replaced with a premium multifocal or trifocal IOL — correcting distance, computer, and reading vision simultaneously. Also called clear lens extraction, it prevents future cataracts as a bonus.

📊

Surface Ablation (PRK)

For patients with thin corneas or active lifestyles where a flap may be undesirable, PRK reshapes the corneal surface without creating a flap. Recovery is slower (5-7 days) but the long-term results are equivalent to LASIK. An excellent option for sportspersons and defence personnel.

Who Is Eligible for Chashma Hatane Ka Operation?

Not everyone is a candidate for every type of refractive surgery — and a surgeon who offers the same procedure to every patient without thorough evaluation is not acting in your best interest. At Sentra Clinic, eligibility is determined through a comprehensive 90-minute pre-operative assessment that includes corneal topography, pachymetry (corneal thickness measurement), pupillometry, tear film evaluation, retinal examination, and wavefront aberrometry. The following general criteria apply:

  • Age: You should be at least 18 years old, with a stable spectacle prescription for at least 12 months. Patients under 18 may still have changing prescriptions, and surgery before stability can lead to regression.
  • Prescription range: LASIK and Contoura Vision typically treat myopia up to -8 dioptres, hyperopia up to +4 dioptres, and astigmatism up to 5 dioptres. ICL can correct up to -20 dioptres of myopia. For extreme prescriptions, lens-based options are usually preferred.
  • Corneal thickness: Laser procedures require adequate corneal tissue. Patients with thin corneas (below 480 microns) may be better suited to SMILE, PRK, or ICL rather than traditional LASIK.
  • Eye health: Active eye infections, severe dry eye, glaucoma, cataracts, or retinal disease must be addressed before refractive surgery. Keratoconus or corneal ectasia are contraindications for laser procedures.
  • General health: Uncontrolled diabetes, autoimmune conditions, and pregnancy can affect healing and prescription stability. We review your full medical history during consultation.
  • Realistic expectations: Refractive surgery reduces or eliminates dependence on glasses, but no procedure guarantees perfect vision for life. Presbyopia (reading glasses after age 40-45) will still develop naturally, and enhancement procedures may occasionally be needed.
Important: A small percentage of patients who request chashma hatane ka operation are not suitable candidates for any laser procedure. If our assessment reveals this, we will tell you honestly and recommend the best alternative — whether that is ICL, refractive lens exchange, or simply continued use of glasses. Your safety comes before any procedure.

Is Chashma Hatane Ka Operation Safe? Understanding the Risks

Safety is the single most important concern for anyone considering refractive surgery, and it should be. The good news is that modern refractive procedures have an extraordinary safety record. Laser vision correction has been performed for over 30 years, with tens of millions of procedures worldwide. The rate of serious complications is less than 1% when surgery is performed by experienced surgeons on properly selected candidates.

However, no surgical procedure is entirely without risk. Potential issues include dry eyes (usually temporary, resolving within weeks to months), glare or halos around lights at night (typically subsiding as healing progresses), under-correction or over-correction (correctable with an enhancement procedure if needed), and in extremely rare cases, infection or corneal flap complications. The risk of each complication is minimised by thorough pre-operative screening, meticulous surgical technique, and careful post-operative care — all of which are standard at Sentra Clinic.

The single most important factor in safety is patient selection. A surgeon who performs refractive surgery on every patient who walks through the door is not prioritising safety. At Sentra Clinic, approximately 15-20% of patients who come seeking chashma hatane ka operation are advised against laser surgery and offered alternatives — because their eyes are not suitable. This honesty is the foundation of our safety record and the trust our patients place in us.

Frequently Asked Questions About Chashma Hatane Ka Operation

Is chashma hatane ka operation painful?

No. All modern refractive procedures are performed under topical anaesthetic eye drops, which numb the surface of the eye completely. You will feel slight pressure and a sensation of touch during the procedure, but no pain. After surgery, you may experience mild discomfort, watering, or a foreign body sensation for a few hours, which resolves on its own. Most patients describe the experience as far easier than a routine dental visit.

How long does the surgery take?

The actual laser treatment time is typically 10-15 seconds per eye for LASIK and Contoura Vision, with the entire procedure including preparation taking 15-20 minutes for both eyes. SMILE takes a similar duration. ICL implantation takes about 20-30 minutes. You will spend approximately one hour at the clinic on the day of surgery, including pre-operative preparation and post-operative observation.

Will I need glasses again after the operation?

The vast majority of patients achieve 6/6 or better distance vision and never need glasses for distance activities again. However, if you are over 40-45, you will still develop presbyopia — the natural age-related need for reading glasses. Refractive lens exchange with multifocal IOLs can address both distance and near vision. Your surgeon will discuss presbyopia management options during your consultation based on your age and visual needs.

What is the minimum age for specs removal surgery?

You must be at least 18 years old, and your spectacle prescription must have been stable for at least 12 months. Most surgeons prefer patients to be 21 or older, as prescriptions tend to stabilise more reliably by then. There is no upper age limit for ICL or refractive lens exchange, provided your eyes are otherwise healthy. LASIK and SMILE are generally performed up to age 40-45, after which lens-based options become more appropriate.

Are there any side effects or risks?

Common temporary side effects include dry eyes, mild glare, halos around lights at night, and slight fluctuations in vision during the first few weeks. These typically resolve within 1-3 months. Serious complications such as infection, corneal flap issues, or significant regression are rare (less than 1%) when surgery is performed by experienced surgeons on properly selected candidates.
